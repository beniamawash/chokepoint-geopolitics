## Papers in This Series

### Paper I — The Tungsten Clock
**Operation Epic Fury and the Material Limits of American War**

> "The United States' doctrine of global primacy presupposes an industrial and organizational base that its own political economy no longer reliably reproduces. Epic Fury is the event that made that presupposition empirically testable rather than merely asserted."

- **File:** `tungsten-clock.html`
- **Status:** Working Paper · Originally circulated March 2026 · Revised June 2026
- **SSRN:** forthcoming

**Abstract**

Operation Epic Fury (28 February – 7 April 2026) exposed the material contradiction at the heart of American power: a doctrine of global primacy sustained by an industrial base hollowed out by decades of deindustrialization and defense-sector financialization. Tungsten functions as a diagnostic clock. China's dominance in tungsten processing and its sequenced export controls converted supply-chain dependence into a strategic timing instrument. This paper documents the burn rate of U.S. precision munitions, the *Time Compression Trap* — the 35–300× asymmetry across three material layers (tungsten, gallium, silicon fabrication stack) — and the *Winchester Trap*: the condition in which a force exhausts years of replenishment capacity against an adversary whose doctrine is built for protracted conflict. It argues that these material limits cannot be resolved through procurement alone and must prompt a deeper reconsideration of grand strategy in an emerging geoeconomic multipolar order.

**Original concepts introduced**

- *Time Compression Trap* — stacked temporal asymmetry across consumptive (tungsten, 35–90×), productive (gallium/GaN, 35–60×), and ecosystem (silicon fabrication, 70–300×) material layers.
- *Winchester Trap* — simultaneous depletion across all three layers against a doctrine built for attrition.
- *Contradiction of Primacy* — the structural gap between hegemonic aspiration and the material base required to sustain it.
- *Organized Denial* — the institutional mechanism by which actors with material stakes in the existing framing prevent structural contradiction from being named.

**Cite as**

Awash, Beniam. *The Tungsten Clock: Operation Epic Fury and the Material Limits of American War.* Working Paper I. March 2026; revised June 2026. Available at: https://beniamawash.github.io/chokepoint-geopolitics/tungsten-clock.html

### Paper II — The Architecture of Denial
**Iran's Coupled Maritime Doctrine at Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb as a Form of Governance**

> "The Strait of Hormuz is not being blockaded — it is being administered. The toll is not a ransom — it is a tariff."

- **File:** `architecture-of-denial.html`
- **Status:** Working Paper · April 2026 · Updated June 2026
- **SSRN:** forthcoming

**Abstract**

This paper argues that Iran has constructed a coupled maritime denial architecture at the Strait of Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb that functions as governance rather than warfare. The IRGC's selective passage regime, codified by Iran's Majlis committee in March 2026 and institutionalized through the Persian Gulf Strait Authority (PGSA) in May 2026, represents the emergence of chokepoint sovereignty — a form of territorial authority that existing international law, UNCLOS doctrine, and freedom-of-navigation frameworks are structurally unprepared to address.

**Original concepts introduced**

- *Tollbooth as Governance Architecture* — Iran's Hormuz transit regime as administrative regulation requiring negotiation, not military defeat.
- *Coupled Chokepoint System* — Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb as multiplicative, not additive, constraints under a single strategic actor.
- *Ostrom Inversion* — enclosure of a global commons through controlled access without formal privatization.
- *Institutional Depth* — the accretion of precedent, infrastructure, and embedded interest making reversal increasingly costly.

**Cite as**

Awash, Beniam. *The Architecture of Denial: Iran's Coupled Maritime Doctrine at Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb as a Form of Governance.* Working Paper II. April 2026; updated June 2026. Available at: https://beniamawash.github.io/chokepoint-geopolitics/architecture-of-denial.html

## Shared Analytical Framework

Both working papers apply Farrell and Newman's weaponized interdependence framework to physical rather than financial networks — chokepoint nodes at the materials layer in Paper I and the maritime geography layer in Paper II. Both treat the institutions responsible for addressing the contradictions they document as part of the analytical object rather than as neutral addressees of policy recommendations. Both argue that the 2026 conflict is best understood through political economy — resource constraints, institutional behavior, and revenue architectures — rather than through standard strategic studies categories.

**Shared theoretical anchors**

- Farrell and Newman, *Weaponized Interdependence* (2019)
- Acharya, Estevadeordal, and Goodman, *Multipolar or Multiplex?* (2023)
- Cooley and Nexon, *Exit from Hegemony* (2020)
- Janis, *Groupthink* (1982); Allison and Zelikow, *Essence of Decision* (1999)
- Ostrom, *Governing the Commons* (1990) — inverted in Paper II

2. Go to **Settings → Pages**.
