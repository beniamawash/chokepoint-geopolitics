# Chokepoint Geopolitics · Working Papers

**Working papers on how control over circulation becomes geopolitical leverage — and who captures the value that circulation generates.**

By **Beniam Awash, PhD**

**Last updated:** August 2026

This repository examines how states convert control over circulation — maritime straits, critical-mineral processing capacity, clearing systems, digital networks — into instruments of coercion, counter-coercion, and value capture.

Live site: [https://beniamawash.github.io/chokepoint-geopolitics/](https://beniamawash.github.io/chokepoint-geopolitics/)

---

## Shared Analytical Framework

Each paper examines a different layer of chokepoint infrastructure:

| Paper | Layer | Focus |
|-------|-------|-------|
| **I** | Materials | Critical minerals as a binding constraint on military and industrial capacity |
| **II** | Maritime Geography | Coupled chokepoint governance as a form of administered denial |
| **III** | Financial / Clearing Systems | The inversion of sanctions architecture into counter-coercive capacity |

The papers extend Farrell and Newman's weaponized interdependence framework (2019) from the financial and informational networks it originally examined to physical, geographic, and materials-based chokepoints — and, in Paper III, to a case the framework does not theorize: the sanctioned target itself acting as a coercive actor. Paper III also builds on Hirschman's (1945) account of coercive self-erosion, in which the exercise of economic leverage motivates the very adaptations designed to erode it.

Each paper's central concept — the Time Compression Trap, the Coupled Chokepoint System, the Sanctions Inversion — is a mid-range, bounded mechanism specified with observable markers, not a general theory of chokepoint power. Each paper is explicit about what a single case, often examined while still unfolding, can and cannot establish, and each documents its claims using a consistent evidence-tiered method (below) that foregrounds institutional behavior, resource constraints, and revenue architecture as the primary explanatory variables.

**Shared theoretical anchors**

- Farrell and Newman, *Weaponized Interdependence* (2019)
- Hirschman, *National Power and the Structure of Foreign Trade* (1945) — extended in Paper III
- Acharya, Estevadeordal, and Goodman, *Multipolar or Multiplex?* (2023)
- Cooley and Nexon, *Exit from Hegemony* (2020)
- Janis, *Groupthink* (1982); Allison and Zelikow, *Essence of Decision* (1999)
- Ostrom, *Governing the Commons* (1990) — inverted in Paper II

## Evidence Tiers

Each claim in every paper is tagged by confidence level:

| Tier | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Confirmed** | Primary source or multiple independent outlets |
| **Reported** | Single credible source, not yet corroborated |
| **Estimated** | Derived from verified data via a stated method |
| **Analyst Inference** | Author's interpretive judgment |

This tiered system makes the evidentiary basis of each argument transparent and contestable. The legend appears at the top of the papers section on the landing page and is applied consistently across all papers in the series.

## Papers in This Series

### Paper I — The Tungsten Clock
**Operation Epic Fury and the Material Limits of American War**

> "The United States' doctrine of global primacy presupposes an industrial and organizational base that its own political economy no longer reliably reproduces. Epic Fury is the event that made that presupposition empirically testable rather than merely asserted."

- **File:** `papers/tungsten-clock.html`
- **Status:** Working Paper · Originally circulated March 2026 · Revised June 2026
- **SSRN:** forthcoming

**Abstract**

Operation Epic Fury (28 February – 7 April 2026) exposed the material contradiction at the heart of American power: a doctrine of global primacy sustained by an industrial base hollowed out by decades of deindustrialization and defense-sector financialization. Tungsten functions as a diagnostic clock. China's dominance in tungsten processing and its sequenced export controls converted supply-chain dependence into a strategic timing instrument. This paper documents the burn rate of U.S. precision munitions, the Time Compression Trap — the 35–300× asymmetry across three material layers (tungsten, gallium, silicon fabrication stack) — and the Winchester Trap: the condition in which a force exhausts years of replenishment capacity against an adversary whose doctrine is built for protracted conflict. It argues that these material limits cannot be resolved through procurement alone and must prompt a deeper reconsideration of grand strategy in an emerging geoeconomic multipolar order.

**Key concepts**
- *Time Compression Trap* — stacked temporal asymmetry across consumptive (tungsten, 35–90×), productive (gallium/GaN, 35–60×), and ecosystem (silicon fabrication, 70–300×) material layers.
- *Winchester Trap* — simultaneous depletion across all three layers against a doctrine built for attrition.
- *Contradiction of Primacy* — the structural gap between hegemonic aspiration and the material base required to sustain it.
- *Organized Denial* — the institutional mechanism by which actors with material stakes in the existing framing prevent structural contradiction from being named.

**Cite as**

Awash, Beniam. *The Tungsten Clock: Operation Epic Fury and the Material Limits of American War.* Chokepoint Geopolitics Working Paper I. March 2026; revised June 2026. Available at: https://beniamawash.github.io/chokepoint-geopolitics/papers/tungsten-clock.html

### Paper II — The Architecture of Denial
**Iran's Coupled Maritime Doctrine at Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb as a Form of Governance**

> "The Strait of Hormuz is not being blockaded — it is being administered. The toll is not a ransom — it is a tariff."

- **File:** `papers/architecture-of-denial.html`
- **Status:** Working Paper · April 2026 · Updated June 2026
- **SSRN:** forthcoming

**Abstract**

This paper argues that Iran has constructed a coupled maritime denial architecture at the Strait of Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb that functions as governance rather than warfare. The IRGC's selective passage regime, codified by Iran's Majlis committee in March 2026 and institutionalized through the Persian Gulf Strait Authority (PGSA) in May 2026, represents the emergence of chokepoint sovereignty — a form of territorial authority that existing international law, UNCLOS doctrine, and freedom-of-navigation frameworks are structurally unprepared to address.

**Key concepts**
- *Tollbooth as Governance Architecture* — Iran's Hormuz transit regime as administrative regulation requiring negotiation, not military defeat.
- *Coupled Chokepoint System* — Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb as multiplicative, not additive, constraints under a single strategic actor.
- *Ostrom Inversion* — enclosure of a global commons through controlled access without formal privatization.
- *Institutional Depth* — the accretion of precedent, infrastructure, and embedded interest making reversal increasingly costly.

**Cite as**

Awash, Beniam. *The Architecture of Denial: Iran's Coupled Maritime Doctrine at Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb as a Form of Governance.* Chokepoint Geopolitics Working Paper II. April 2026; updated June 2026. Available at: https://beniamawash.github.io/chokepoint-geopolitics/papers/architecture-of-denial.html

### Paper III — The Sanctions Inversion
**Chokepoint Power and the Limits of Dollar Coercion**

> "The 2026 Hormuz crisis provides the first large-scale empirical instance of that case."

- **File:** `papers/sanctions-inversion.html`
- **Status:** Working Paper · Originally circulated March 2026 · Revised June 10, 2026 · Postscript added June 14, 2026
- **SSRN:** forthcoming

**Abstract**

This paper introduces the Sanctions Inversion, a mid-range concept in the political economy of financial statecraft: the process by which a sanctioned state converts physical chokepoint control into counter-sanctions, alternative financial infrastructure, and selective access governance. Building on Hirschman's insight that coercion generates the adaptations designed to erode it, and extending Farrell and Newman's weaponized interdependence framework to the case of the target state as coercive actor, the paper argues that the 2026 Hormuz crisis exhibits the mechanism's observable components. Iran's selective transit regime, yuan-denominated toll architecture, and quota-based access governance are not separate responses to military pressure but sequential components of a single counter-coercive strategy. Together, they show how sustained sanctions pressure can produce the financial infrastructure that weakens the dollar coercion generating it. Its central claim is bounded but consequential: under specific structural conditions — comprehensive sanctions, physical chokepoint control at the cross-domain intersection of energy flow and financial clearing, and exhausted passive endurance — the target of economic coercion can become a provider of system-critical access, reversing the direction of dependence that sanctions are designed to exploit. This paper examines events through June 10, 2026; the conflict remains ongoing and the analysis offered here is provisional. A postscript added June 14, 2026 addresses the announced US-Iran memorandum of understanding and its bearing on the paper's central open question.

**Key concepts**
- *Sanctions Inversion* — a three-dimensional mechanism (deterrence, conversion, institutionalization) by which a sanctioned state converts physical chokepoint control into counter-sanctions, financial alternative-building, and selective access governance.
- *Coercive Self-Erosion* — building on Hirschman (1945): the exercise of economic leverage motivates the very adaptations designed to erode it.
- *Cross-Domain Chokepoint* — the structural condition required for inversion: a chokepoint sitting at the intersection of a physical flow dependency and a financial clearing dependency simultaneously.
- Distinguishes *sanctions evasion*, *sanctions resilience*, and *sanctions inversion* as three analytically separate responses to comprehensive sanctions pressure, of which inversion is the rarest and most structurally consequential.

**Cite as**

Awash, Beniam. *Sanctions Inversion: Chokepoint Power and the Limits of Dollar Coercion.* Chokepoint Geopolitics Working Paper III. March 2026; revised June 10, 2026; postscript added June 14, 2026. Available at: https://beniamawash.github.io/chokepoint-geopolitics/papers/sanctions-inversion.html

## About the Author

Beniam Awash, PhD, studies how states turn control over **circulation** into leverage over one another — and who captures the value that circulation generates along the way. His research spans carbon flows, critical minerals, financial systems, and maritime corridors.

He holds a PhD in Sociology from Binghamton University. Current work extends the **Circulatory Sovereignty** framework — the site's organizing lens for this series — to digital infrastructure and compute chokepoints.
